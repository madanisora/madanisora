## About Me

I'm Harits Akbar Al Madhani (MadaniSora), an independent security researcher interested in understanding software vulnerabilities and developing practical methods to discover and analyze them.

My long-term research interests include:

- **Systems Security:** operating-system internals, Linux, and virtualization security.
- **Automated Vulnerability Discovery:** fuzzing, test-case generation, crash analysis, and root-cause investigation.
- **Binary Analysis:** reverse engineering, debugging, and memory-safety issues.
- **Smart Contract Security:** code auditing and invariant-focused safety analysis.

I'm building a research-oriented portfolio that emphasizes technical evidence, reproducibility, and honest reporting of results.

## Research Interests

My primary direction is automated vulnerability discovery in low-level systems software.

I'm particularly interested in how fuzzing, program analysis, and systematic crash investigation can help researchers discover software defects, understand their root causes, and evaluate potential security impact.

These are ongoing research interests, not claims of original vulnerability discoveries or completed research findings.

## Technical Portfolio

I use GitHub to document technical work in a way that other people can inspect and reproduce.

The portfolio is intended to include:

- **CTF & Lab Write-ups:** challenge objectives, methodology, solutions, and lessons learned, published only when the applicable rules permit.
- **Security Experiments:** test setup, hypotheses, procedures, observations, results, and limitations.
- **Tools & Scripts:** small utilities and automation that support analysis and reproducible testing.
- **Research Notes:** technical explanations, references, experiment logs, and questions for further investigation.

Learning exercises, reproduced results, and original findings are identified separately. A crash or unexpected program behavior is not described as a confirmed vulnerability without adequate validation.

## Research Principles

- Reproducibility over unsupported claims.
- Evidence-based analysis over speculation.
- Clear distinction between observations, hypotheses, and conclusions.
- Responsible testing only in authorized environments.
- Proper attribution to original research, tools, and references.
